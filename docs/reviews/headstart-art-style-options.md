## Findings and acceptance

**PASS — distinct requested alternatives.** C6 visibly uses paper texture, cut edges, folded birds and faceted depth planes. C7 uses rounded clay towers, modeled clouds/trees and a sculpted helper. C8 translates the scenery and companion into pixel clusters while keeping high-resolution readable interface type. C9 supplies a fourth, clearly different editorial engraving/collage treatment with serif headings, ink hatching, cyan fields and a yellow sun. These are changes of visual medium, not merely recoloring.

**PASS — wider original-style composition.** C10 retains the sunlit stylized 3D-like look, but shows a smaller companion and more distant castle amid expanded sea, islands, villages and paths. The website headline, navigation, form and main actions retain their readable scale. It is correctly described as a composition variation rather than another art medium.

**PASS — purpose and continuity.** All variants retain HeadStart's game-remix explanation, prompt, primary action, browse route, bag and pause affordance. The recognizable blue-caped companion remains connected to the path/cursor cue. Art does not obstruct critical controls. C9 deliberately changes type treatment while retaining hierarchy.

**PASS — honest scope and provenance.** All new images visibly disclose generated concepts and no video/motion. The notes correctly state that these are flattened rasters, not layered exports or interactive backgrounds. Every influence is framed as an interpretation, without a copied wordmark or claim of endorsement. The options document records still-art-first scope and future motion separation; no executed Replicate video call, payment or generated clip is asserted.

Nonblocking design observations: C8's detailed ground is visually busier; broad discovery imagery will help avoid suggesting a retro-only catalog. C9's surreal floating architecture will need a coherent reachable path if implemented. C10 intentionally reduces companion prominence; mobile scaling needs later review. These are editorial tradeoffs, not measured usability findings.
