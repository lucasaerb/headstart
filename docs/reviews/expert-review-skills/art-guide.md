# Harbor garden — editable art guide

**Editorial review of a first-party fixture, not a HeadStart redesign.** Rubric `headstart-review-rubric-1`; fixture brief revision 1, source version `expert-review-fixture/1`. Source and temporary target commits/capture hashes are in [evidence.json](evidence.json). The integration adapter is not involved in this independent fixture review. Review-only inspection left the target byte-identical; the separate bounded correction changed only `game.mjs` and preserved the unrelated file and notice.

The brief asks for a quiet coastal garden with muted colors and rounded readable silhouettes. I inspected the real desktop before/after captures and mobile after capture. The initial center tower has an intensely magenta rectangle and almost-black disconnected triangular spikes. These dominate the muted blue/sage/cream scene, and the disconnected roof reads as floating shards. This observation is visible in [desktop before](captures/desktop-before.png); the choice to unify it is an editorial response to this particular brief, not a universal objection to saturated colors or sharp shapes.

| Area | Proposed editable direction | Evidence, alternative and tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette | Sky `#c6dfdf`, water `#96bdc9`, ground `#87ad86`, tower ochre `#dfac5b`, roof slate `#566e88`, warm-cream windows. | The before tower's `#f02cdd` breaks the surrounding muted palette. Ochre retains a warm focal point without the neon contrast. Keeping magenta could be appropriate for a hazardous objective, but that is absent from this brief. |
| Geometry | Rounded tower corners, one continuous domed roof; retain simple circular trees and neighboring triangular cottage roofs. | The before black zigzag roof has disconnected-looking facets. A joined dome removes that accidental fragmentation. Making every roof round would erase the useful tower/cottage distinction. |
| Camera and composition | Retain the existing wide orthographic-like 2D view, central tower and two flanking houses. | Actual before/after use the identical 960×540 canvas and framing. A closer crop could make interaction details more readable but reduce the calm environmental context. |
| Lighting and contrast | Flat diffuse lighting and restrained value range, with dark foliage/roof for structure. | The fixture uses flat canvas fills, not a 3D lighting rig. Do not claim physically based lighting. Subtle shadows could improve depth at the cost of a less graphic treatment; not part of this bounded change. |
| Materials and textures | Matte, untextured color blocks; keep texture frequency low if assets are added later. | No texture bytes or materials are loaded. Do not infer memory savings or FPS from this choice. A light paper texture is an editorial alternative requiring its own display rights and mobile-legibility check. |
| Animation | Static for this inspection. If requested later, begin with slow water/bird accents and a reduced-motion alternative. | No animation was implemented or measured in the fixture. Constant tower motion could undermine the quiet mood; no such change was authorized here. |
| Dimension and asset workflow | Stay with 2D procedural shapes for this fixture; no external assets or Blender pipeline. | A 3D scene could support camera exploration, but introduces modeling, lighting and camera work absent from this brief. Shape coherence does not prove 3D compatibility. |

The authorized correction changes `art.accent`, `art.roof` and `art.shape` in [after.mjs](../../../tools/review/fixtures/after.mjs:2). [Desktop after](captures/desktop-after.png) keeps the warm center focal point while the slate roof belongs to the surrounding scene. [Mobile after](captures/mobile-after.png) retains three distinct building silhouettes without overflow or cropped text. All shapes are authored fixture artwork; no upstream game images or new HeadStart art direction are being proposed.

Remaining editorial limit: the tower is still deliberately larger and warmer than the houses. That hierarchy fits this brief, but only real gameplay/user feedback could establish whether the tower is the right interaction focus. No playability, measured performance or source-reuse readiness follows from this art review.
